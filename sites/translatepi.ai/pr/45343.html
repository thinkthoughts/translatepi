Euler's totient over pairwise coprime products

A reusable finite-product identity, where the distinct-prime case follows as a corollary.

Status: under review. The proposed Lean declarations reportedly passed focused local compilation and lint checks. They have not yet been merged into Mathlib. View PR #45343.

1. What does the totient count?

Euler's totient function \(\varphi(n)\) counts the integers from \(1\) through \(n\) whose greatest common divisor with \(n\) is \(1\). Two integers are coprime if their greatest common divisor is \(1\).

\[\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\varphi(9)=6}.\]

2. From two factors to many

Mathlib already establishes multiplicativity for two coprime factors with Nat.totient_mul:

\[\gcd(a,b)=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)}.\]

For example, \(4\) and \(9\) are coprime, and \(\varphi(36)=12=\varphi(4)\varphi(9)=2\cdot6\).

The proposed theorem extends this rule to a finite indexed family \(f(i)\), provided factors at distinct indices are pairwise coprime:

\[\Big(\forall i\ne j\in s,\ \gcd(f(i),f(j))=1\Big)\Longrightarrow \boxed{\varphi\!\left(\prod_{i\in s}f(i)\right)=\prod_{i\in s}\varphi(f(i))}.\]

Proposed Lean declaration: Nat.totient_prod_of_pairwise_coprime. Taking \(f(i)=i\) recovers the ordinary finite-set form.

The factors may be composite. For \(4,9,25\), every pair is coprime:

\[\varphi(900)=\varphi(4)\varphi(9)\varphi(25)=2\cdot6\cdot20=\boxed{240}.\]

3. Why the proof works

  1. Empty collection: its product is \(1\), and \(\varphi(1)=1\).
  2. Induction: assume the identity for an existing collection.
  3. Add a new index \(a\notin s\): pairwise coprimality makes \(f(a)\) coprime to the product of all earlier factors.
  4. Multiply totients: apply Nat.totient_mul, then the induction hypothesis.

Lean formalizes these steps with finset induction and established coprimality lemmas.

4. Distinct primes are a special case

Every prime \(p\) has \(\varphi(p)=p-1\), and distinct primes are coprime. A finite set lists every element once, so a set containing only primes satisfies the general theorem's hypothesis.

\[\boxed{\varphi\!\left(\prod_{p\in s}p\right)=\prod_{p\in s}(p-1)}.\]

Proposed Lean declaration: Nat.totient_prod_of_forall_prime. The prime corollary is stated as its own theorem for downstream reuse; its proof invokes the general result without repeating the induction.

\[\varphi(30)=(2-1)(3-1)(5-1)=\boxed{8}.\]

The eight mod-30 residue representatives coprime to 30 are

\[\boxed{\{1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29\}}.\]

This links the theorem to the mod-30 reading lanes in the General Divisor Theorem project. Coprimality to 30 is a residue condition; it does not imply primality.

5. What the contribution specifies

The mathematics is classical. The contribution is a directly reusable formal theorem about finite products, plus a prime-specific corollary. It records a general multiplicative invariant once, so later Lean developments can invoke it without reconstructing the induction.
