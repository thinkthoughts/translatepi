01 / The paper

What we already wrote

In A Structural Reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis via Invariance and Projection, we introduced three names for operations on complex numbers and the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function. The paper establishes a structural reformulation and an elementary equivalence argument for the standard RH statement.

Nontrivial zeros
Z = { s ∈ ℂ : ζ(s) = 0 and 0 < Re(s) < 1 }

left4(s)

Take the real part: left4(s) := Re(s).

left5(Z)

Select only the zeros on the critical line: {s ∈ Z : Re(s) = ½}.

lift5(s)

Say that a nontrivial zero satisfies the critical-line constraint: Re(s) = ½.

Invariance and symmetry

Prime factorization, the Euler product, and zero symmetry provide context. The critical-line condition adds the assertion that every nontrivial zero lies on the symmetry line.

02 / Exact mathematical connection

Two expressions, one proposition

Our projection form
Z = left5(Z)
Conventional RH form
∀s ∈ ℂ, (ζ(s) = 0 ∧ 0 < Re(s) < 1) → Re(s) = ½

Because left5(Z) is defined as the subset of Z whose elements have real part ½, the equality means every element of Z satisfies that condition. Unfolding the definitions establishes equivalence between these two expressions. The externally verified RH proof addresses the mathematical proposition itself.

A formal comparison must also resolve the precise zeta function, nontrivial-zero predicate, coercions, complex-number conventions, and quantifiers used by the target Lean declaration.
