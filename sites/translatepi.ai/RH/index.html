01 / The paper

What we already wrote

In A Structural Reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis via Invariance and Projection, we introduced three names for operations on complex numbers and the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function. The paper explicitly presents a reformulation, not a proof of RH.

Nontrivial zeros
Z = { s ∈ ℂ : ζ(s) = 0 and 0 < Re(s) < 1 }

left4(s)

Take the real part: left4(s) := Re(s).

left5(Z)

Select only the zeros on the critical line: {s ∈ Z : Re(s) = ½}.

lift5(s)

Say that a nontrivial zero satisfies the critical-line constraint: Re(s) = ½.

Invariance and symmetry

Prime factorization, the Euler product, and zero symmetry provide context. Symmetry alone does not prove that all zeros lie on the line.

02 / Exact mathematical connection

Two expressions, one proposition

Our projection form
Z = left5(Z)
Conventional RH form
∀s ∈ ℂ, (ζ(s) = 0 ∧ 0 < Re(s) < 1) → Re(s) = ½

Because left5(Z) is defined as the subset of Z whose elements have real part ½, the equality means every element of Z satisfies that condition. This establishes the ordinary mathematical equivalence by unfolding the definitions; it does not establish the truth of either statement.

A formal comparison must also resolve the precise zeta function, nontrivial-zero predicate, coercions, complex-number conventions, and quantifiers used by the target Lean declaration.

05 / What this does not claim

A translation is not another RH proof

Our reformulation does not prove RH, provide new zero bounds, or establish that the reported RH proof uses our operators. The PiπΠ reading framework helps us distinguish imagination, expression, and constraints; it is not a substitute for the mathematical theorem. Our RLT prover's present Robinson's Q work does not independently replay a complex-analysis proof of RH.
