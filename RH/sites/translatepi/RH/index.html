03 / Mathematical translation · PiπΠ

RH. Read the statement across notations.

A proof must preserve the mathematical claim as it moves between words, symbols, formal definitions, and machine-checked theorems. Translation is a proof obligation, not a typographic resemblance.

∀ρ, ζ(ρ) = 0 ∧ 0 < Re(ρ) < 1 ⇒ Re(ρ) = ½

On November 21, 2026, the Lean and Mathlib communities jointly certified the statement and proof in Levent Alpöge’s Lean formalization of the Riemann Hypothesis. This page records that external verification, not a new RLT proof or an independent replay by this site.

01 / Context

Which statement survives translation?

The zeta-function assertion can be written in prose, complex-variable notation, or Lean. Those are distinct inscriptions. The central question is whether the chosen definitions and translations preserve exactly the same proposition, with the same quantifiers and restrictions.

02 / Reading points

Four distinct checks

Pi · imagination

Identify candidate interpretations and questions worth formalizing. Imaginative alternatives are inputs to investigation, not verified conclusions.

π · expression and extension

Specify the mathematical objects, maps, and formal declarations. Preserve the difference between a notation and its intended definition.

Π · constraints and resistance

Require each claimed equivalence to survive explicit comparison, proof checking, and dependency inspection.

Nearby research, distinct result

Constrained-simplex integration, Gram certificates, and prime-gap research belong to the site’s existing mathematics. They do not by themselves establish RH.

03 / Work ahead

A translation record, not a second RH proof

A useful RLT–RH translation record preserves the original claim, the target formal statement, the comparison method, the checker outputs, and the remaining interpretation obligations. PiπΠ is a reading framework here, not a claimed replacement for the zeta-function theorem.
